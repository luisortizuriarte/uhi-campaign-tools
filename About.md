The Summer 2026 Northern Virginia Urban Heat Island Mapping Campaign was a citizen-science initiative launched to identify hyper-local temperature disparities across Fairfax County, Arlington County, and the City of Alexandria. 

Conducted on July 16,  during one of the season's hottest stretches, the project was organized through a partnership between George Mason University’s Virginia Climate Center (VCC) and the regional nonprofit Faith Alliance for Climate Solutions (FACS).

## About the measurements
More than 80 volunteers organized into over 30 teams. Each team mounted portable thermal and humidity sensors to their vehicle roofs.Teams drove 30 pre-selected routes across urban corridors, commercial strips, and residential neighborhoods during three coordinated intervals (morning, afternoon, and evening) to track how heat built up and lingered throughout the day. The study specifically targeted neighborhoods with dense asphalt and low tree canopy coverage. Organizers emphasized identifying vulnerable communities, such as residents facing high energy burdens or outdoor workers at heightened risk of heat-related illness.

## What you are seeing here
Researchers at the Virginia Climate Center and Virginia State Climate Office processed the measurements through a machine-learning combined with satellite imagery to produce the maps you see here. The maps show a snapshot of the temperatures on July 16 2026, highlighting the hot spots in the region and their relationship to their surroundings.
